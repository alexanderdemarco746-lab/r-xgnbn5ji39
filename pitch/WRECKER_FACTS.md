# Wrecker facts: the items that ruin a load of recycling or damage the plant

Checked on 2026-11-21. Every bullet below comes from a page that was opened and read on that date. Each bullet gives: the number, a sentence you can say out loud, the source, the URL, the year of the data, and "opened today: yes". Items that could not be opened are not used as facts; they are listed at the end.

Bin rules differ from place to place. Where a bullet is about one city or county, it says so and cites that city's or county's own page (or says clearly when it is a news report).

## (a) Lithium batteries, vapes and electronics: fires in trucks and plants

- **Number:** more than 240 fires at 64 facilities, between 2013 and 2020.
  **Say it:** "The US EPA found more than 240 fires caused by lithium-ion batteries at 64 waste facilities between 2013 and 2020. That count covers sorting plants, garbage and recycling trucks, landfills and other sites."
  **Source:** US EPA, page "The Importance of Sending Consumers' Used Lithium-ion Batteries to Electronic Recyclers or Hazardous Waste Collection Facilities" (summarizes EPA's report on lithium-ion battery fires; page last updated July 27, 2026).
  **URL:** https://www.epa.gov/recycle/importance-sending-consumers-used-lithium-ion-batteries-electronic-recyclers-or-hazardous
  **Year of data:** 2013 to 2020. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number:** 430 publicly reported fires in 2024, next to 373 in 2023.
  **Say it:** "Fire Rover counted 430 publicly reported fires at waste and recycling facilities in the US and Canada in 2024. The year before, it counted 373."
  **Note:** these are only the fires that made it into public reports, so it is an undercount. The same article says fires at sorting plants and transfer stations rose 20% in 2024 over 2023.
  **Source:** Fire Rover annual count (Ryan Fogelman), as reported by Resource Recycling, "Recycling facility fires increase substantially in 2024", March 18, 2025.
  **URL:** https://resource-recycling.com/recycling/2025/03/18/recycling-facility-fires-increase-substantially-in-2024/
  **Year of data:** 2023 and 2024. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number:** 1.2 billion vapes a year (an estimate).
  **Say it:** "Fire Rover's report estimates that 1.2 billion vapes enter waste and recycling streams each year, and its author names disposable vapes as one possible reason fires went up."
  **Source:** Fire Rover report, as reported by Resource Recycling, March 18, 2025.
  **URL:** https://resource-recycling.com/recycling/2025/03/18/recycling-facility-fires-increase-substantially-in-2024/
  **Year of data:** 2024 report year. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number:** more than 5,000 fires a year at recycling facilities (an estimate).
  **Say it:** "The National Waste and Recycling Association estimates that more than 5,000 fires happen at recycling facilities every year. Its report is about the growing threat from lithium batteries."
  **Source:** National Waste & Recycling Association (NWRA) and Resource Recycling Systems (RRS), news release of January 10, 2024.
  **URL:** https://wasterecycling.org/news-releases/nwra-and-rrs-release-report-on-threat-of-lithium-batteries-to-waste-and-recycling-infrastructure/
  **Year of data:** report released January 2024. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number:** insurance went from under 20 cents per $100 of insured property value to as much as $10 per $100; the rate of catastrophic losses rose 41% over five years.
  **Say it:** "Insuring a recycling plant used to cost under 20 cents for every $100 of property. Now it can cost as much as $10 for every $100."
  **Source:** NWRA and RRS news release, January 10, 2024.
  **URL:** https://wasterecycling.org/news-releases/nwra-and-rrs-release-report-on-threat-of-lithium-batteries-to-waste-and-recycling-infrastructure/
  **Year of data:** report released January 2024. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number:** over $1 billion a year (one expert's estimate).
  **Say it:** "The person who runs the Fire Rover fire count estimated in 2022 that battery-related fires cost the waste industry over $1 billion a year."
  **Note:** this is an estimate by Ryan Fogelman of Fire Rover, not an official figure. In the same article he projected over 400 reported waste facility fires in the US and Canada for 2022 and said the real number could be six times as high.
  **Source:** The National Desk (published by WJLA), "Tiny batteries can be big fire risk for recyclers, landfills", September 15, 2022.
  **URL:** https://wjla.com/news/nation-world/tiny-batteries-can-be-big-fire-risk-for-recyclers-landfills-lithium-ion-batteries-recycling-center-fires-epa
  **Year of data:** 2022. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number (Hillsborough County, Florida):** over 30 truck fires in three years.
  **Say it:** "Hillsborough County Solid Waste says it had over 30 garbage and recycling truck fires over the past three years. It said so in a release warning that lithium-ion batteries are a fire risk in the garbage or recycling."
  **Source:** Hillsborough County, FL news release, "Lithium-Ion Batteries Pose a Fire Risk When Disposed in the Garbage or Recycling", December 9, 2025.
  **URL:** https://hcfl.gov/newsroom/2025/12/09/lithium-ion-batteries-pose-a-fire-risk-when-disposed-in-the-garbage-or-recycling
  **Year of data:** the three years up to December 2025. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number (Hillsborough County, Florida):** one garbage truck a total loss; seven "hot loads" since December 2022.
  **Say it:** "A Hillsborough County garbage truck was a total loss after a fire, and the county reported seven hot loads in its garbage trucks since December 2022. The county lists cell phone lithium-ion batteries, aerosol cans and flammable chemicals as common causes of garbage fires."
  **Note:** the county says the actual cause of that truck fire is unknown. The page gives the fire's date as April 24 without a year.
  **Source:** Hillsborough County, FL news release, "Dangerous Garbage Fires are Increasing, but There Are Ways to Prevent Them" (page posted March 22, 2024).
  **URL:** https://hcfl.gov/newsroom/2024/03/22/dangerous-garbage-fires-are-increasing-but-there-are-ways-to-prevent-them
  **Year of data:** December 2022 onward. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number (Florida, what a facility fire can do):** a fire that burned for two weeks.
  **Say it:** "At a waste-to-energy plant in Doral, Florida, a fire burned for two weeks, destroyed two large portions of the facility and closed two schools because of air quality."
  **Note:** the county page does not say what started that fire. Use it only to show how serious a waste facility fire is, not as a battery example.
  **Source:** Hillsborough County, FL news release (same page as above).
  **URL:** https://hcfl.gov/newsroom/2024/03/22/dangerous-garbage-fires-are-increasing-but-there-are-ways-to-prevent-them
  **Year of data:** not stated on the page. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number:** 68% of young disposable-vape users threw empties in the trash; 13% put them in regular recycling bins.
  **Say it:** "In a Truth Initiative survey of 15-to-24-year-olds, 68% of disposable vape users threw their empty vapes in the trash and 13% put them in regular recycling bins. The lithium batteries inside can explode or start fires in recycling bins, waste trucks or waste processing plants."
  **Source:** Truth Initiative, "Most young users put disposable e-cigarettes in trash, creating huge streams of toxic and hazardous waste", December 15, 2022.
  **URL:** https://truthinitiative.org/research-resources/harmful-effects-tobacco/most-young-users-put-disposable-e-cigarettes-trash
  **Year of data:** 2022. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number:** 36% throw batteries in the trash; 30% mix them with other recyclables.
  **Say it:** "In a survey of 4,032 US adults, 36% said they throw batteries in the trash and 30% said they mix them in with other recyclables."
  **Source:** UL Standards & Engagement, "Consumer Perceptions on Sustainability & Energy Transition", August 20, 2024.
  **URL:** https://ulse.org/insight/data-insights-consumer-perceptions-sustainability-energy-transition/
  **Year of data:** published 2024. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number:** 19% of US adults throw old devices away.
  **Say it:** "In a CNET survey of 2,511 US adults, nearly one in five, 19%, said they throw away old devices such as smartphones."
  **Note:** the CNET page itself could not be opened; the figure was read in Supply Chain Connect's report of that survey.
  **Source:** CNET survey by YouGov, as reported by Supply Chain Connect, May 7, 2025.
  **URL:** https://www.supplychainconnect.com/news-trends/article/55287926/shining-a-light-on-electronics-hoarding-and-e-waste
  **Year of data:** 2025. **Opened today: yes**

- **What the official pages tell people to do (no number):**
  **Say it:** "The EPA says lithium-ion batteries should not go in household trash or recycling bins. They should go to a household hazardous waste collection site or an electronics recycler."
  **Careful:** Hillsborough County's page says rechargeable and lithium-ion batteries must go to a drop-off, but "Regular alkaline batteries, such as AA or AAA, can safely go in the trash." The City of Tampa's page says "Electronics or batteries of any kind are never to be placed in your recycling cart." So these pages treat lithium-ion and rechargeable batteries as the fire risk, and Hillsborough County says ordinary alkaline AAs are safe in the trash.
  **Sources and URLs:**
  US EPA (page last updated July 27, 2026): https://www.epa.gov/recycle/importance-sending-consumers-used-lithium-ion-batteries-electronic-recyclers-or-hazardous
  Hillsborough County, FL (December 9, 2025): https://hcfl.gov/newsroom/2025/12/09/lithium-ion-batteries-pose-a-fire-risk-when-disposed-in-the-garbage-or-recycling
  City of Tampa, Recycling FAQs (updated 02/06/2025): https://tampa.gov/node/75506
  **Year of data:** 2025 and 2026 pages. **Opened today: yes**

## (b) Plastic bags and film: jamming the sorting machines

- **Number (City of Tampa):** up to 3 hours of shutdowns each day.
  **Say it:** "The City of Tampa says tanglers, which means plastic film, bags, clothes, ropes, cords and hoses, cause shutdowns in the sorting process for up to 3 hours each day."
  **Source:** City of Tampa, "Recycling FAQs" (updated 02/06/2025).
  **URL:** https://tampa.gov/node/75506
  **Year of data:** page updated 2025. **Opened today: yes**

- **What Hillsborough County tells residents (no number):**
  **Say it:** "Hillsborough County says plastic bags, cords, wires, tarps and hoses jam and damage sorting equipment, and that bagged recyclables are not recycled because the equipment cannot sort them."
  **Also on that page:** plastic bags can only be recycled at participating retailers, and if you cannot return them to a store it is okay to put them in the garbage.
  **Source:** Hillsborough County, FL, "Tampa Bay Recycles" (last modified 10/15/2025).
  **URL:** https://hcfl.gov/residents/sustainability-and-green/recycling/tampa-bay-recycles
  **Year of data:** page modified 2025. **Opened today: yes**

- **What the City of Tampa's brochure says (no number):**
  **Say it:** "Tampa's recycling brochure names plastic bags and plastic wrap among the unaccepted items that commonly get placed in the recycling cart, and says contaminants can shut down equipment, create safety hazards for workers, raise the cost of recycling and ruin good recyclables."
  **Source:** City of Tampa, "Curbside Recycling Guidelines" brochure, dated 03-2026.
  **URL:** https://www.tampa.gov/sites/default/files/document/2026/recycling-brochure-2026-03.pdf
  **Year of data:** 2026. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number (Tampa, news report of a city audit):** plastic bags and plastic wrap: 34 percent (2,531 counted); bagged recyclables: 9 percent (693 counted).
  **Say it:** "When Tampa audited the recycling from 5,000 homes for five weeks, plastic bags and plastic wrap topped the list of things that should not have been there, at 34 percent."
  **Note:** this is FOX 13's report of the city's audit, not a city web page. The article lists the percentages for "the most common items that get tossed in the recycling when they shouldn't"; it does not spell out what total they are a share of.
  **Source:** FOX 13 Tampa Bay, "What is and isn't recyclable? Tampa city officials hold demonstration to educate residents", September 28, 2021.
  **URL:** https://www.fox13news.com/news/what-is-and-isnt-recyclable-tampa-city-officials-hold-demonstration-to-educate-residents
  **Year of data:** reported 2021. **Opened today: yes**

- **What a national hauler says (no number):**
  **Say it:** "The waste hauler WM says tanglers wrap around equipment and can shut down an entire facility, and it lists recyclables in plastic bags among the most common contaminants."
  **Source:** WM (Waste Management), "Most Common Contaminants" poster, 2026.
  **URL:** https://www.wm.com/content/dam/wm/documents/RecyclingResources/Posters-Guides-Tools/Poster-Most-Common-Contaminants-093026.pdf
  **Year of data:** 2026. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number:** 61% of US adults said flexible plastic can go in their curbside bin; 41% said so for plastic grocery bags.
  **Say it:** "In a 2022 survey of 2,011 US adults, 61% said flexible plastic can go in their curbside recycling bin and 41% said plastic grocery bags can. The survey marks both as generally not recyclable there."
  **Source:** Republic Services Consumer Recycling Survey, September 2022.
  **URL:** https://www.republicservices.com/cms/documents/Documents/AmericaRecyclesDay/America-Recycles-Day-Survey-Data-2022.pdf
  **Year of data:** 2022. **Opened today: yes**

## (c) Contamination overall: how much of what goes in recycling is the wrong thing

- **Number (City of Tampa):** 63% recyclable, 37% contamination.
  **Say it:** "The City of Tampa said that only about 63% of what is placed in recycling bins in Tampa is recyclable. The remaining 37% is contamination."
  **Source:** City of Tampa news release, "City of Tampa Recycling Composition Study", posted 11/18/2020 (it cites "a previous composition study").
  **URL:** https://www.tampa.gov/news/city-tampa-recycling-composition-study
  **Year of data:** release 2020; the year of the earlier study is not stated. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number (Tampa, news report):** 64% recyclable on average.
  **Say it:** "After auditing 5,000 homes, Tampa officials told FOX 13 that on average only 64% of the items residents put in their recycling containers are actually recyclable. Plain trash was second on the list of wrong items, at 32 percent (2,364 counted)."
  **Note:** FOX 13's report of the city's audit, not a city web page.
  **Source:** FOX 13 Tampa Bay, September 28, 2021.
  **URL:** https://www.fox13news.com/news/what-is-and-isnt-recyclable-tampa-city-officials-hold-demonstration-to-educate-residents
  **Year of data:** reported 2021. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number (City of Tampa):** contamination "at all-time high"; over 3.6 million pounds recycled in 12 months.
  **Say it:** "In November 2022 the City of Tampa said recycling contamination was at an all-time high, in a year when residents and businesses recycled over 3.6 million pounds of material."
  **Source:** City of Tampa news release, "Recycling Contamination at All-Time High", posted 11/14/2022.
  **URL:** https://www.tampa.gov/news/recycling-contamination-all-time-high-110031
  **Year of data:** 2022. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number (national):** more than $300 million each year.
  **Say it:** "The Recycling Partnership estimated that contamination costs the US recycling system more than $300 million each year."
  **Source:** The Recycling Partnership, "Updated Report: The West Coast Contamination Initiative", April 30, 2020.
  **URL:** https://recyclingpartnership.org/?p=30744
  **Year of data:** 2019 study, published 2020. **Opened today: yes**

- **Official statement (no number):**
  **Say it:** "The EPA says non-recyclable items can contaminate a whole load of recyclables, causing all of it to be thrown out."
  **Source:** US EPA, "Frequent Questions about Recycling" (last updated January 22, 2026).
  **URL:** https://www.epa.gov/recycle/frequent-questions-about-recycling
  **Year of data:** page updated 2026. **Opened today: yes**

- **Official statement (Hillsborough County, no number):**
  **Say it:** "Hillsborough County says contamination increases recycling costs and degrades the quality of materials, which reduces the ability of the materials to be recycled."
  **Source:** Hillsborough County, FL, "Tampa Bay Recycles" (last modified 10/15/2025).
  **URL:** https://hcfl.gov/residents/sustainability-and-green/recycling/tampa-bay-recycles
  **Year of data:** page modified 2025. **Opened today: yes**

- **What counts as contamination (no number):**
  **Say it:** "WM says a container is contaminated when the recycling bin contains trash, when the materials are soiled by food or liquids, or when items are placed in plastic bags."
  **Source:** WM (Waste Management), "What is contamination and why is it such a big deal?"
  **URL:** https://www.wm.com/us/en/support/faqs/products-and-services/what-is-contamination-and-why-is-it-such-a-big-deal
  **Year of data:** page as read in 2026. **Opened today: yes**

## (d) Food and liquid: spoiling paper and cardboard

- **Official statement (City of Tampa): a whole truckload can be rejected.**
  **Say it:** "The City of Tampa says liquid and food residue affect the quality of the material collected and can cause the entire truck load to be rejected."
  **Source:** City of Tampa, "Recycling FAQs" (updated 02/06/2025).
  **URL:** https://tampa.gov/node/75506
  **Year of data:** page updated 2025. **Opened today: yes**

- **Official rule (City of Tampa): no wet or food-soiled cardboard.**
  **Say it:** "Tampa's brochure says recyclables must be dry, empty, clean and rinsed. For cardboard it says no wet cardboard and no pizza or soiled food boxes."
  **Source:** City of Tampa, "Curbside Recycling Guidelines" brochure, dated 03-2026.
  **URL:** https://www.tampa.gov/sites/default/files/document/2026/recycling-brochure-2026-03.pdf
  **Year of data:** 2026. **Opened today: yes**

- **Official statement (US EPA): food-soiled paper never goes in recycling.**
  **Say it:** "The EPA's list of things you should never put in a recycling bin includes food and food-soiled paper."
  **Source:** US EPA, "Frequent Questions about Recycling" (last updated January 22, 2026).
  **URL:** https://www.epa.gov/recycle/frequent-questions-about-recycling
  **Year of data:** page updated 2026. **Opened today: yes**

- **Official statement (Hillsborough County):**
  **Say it:** "Hillsborough County says unrinsed food containers and soiled paper cannot be recovered, and they contaminate the other clean recyclables."
  **Source:** Hillsborough County, FL, "Tampa Bay Recycles" (last modified 10/15/2025).
  **URL:** https://hcfl.gov/residents/sustainability-and-green/recycling/tampa-bay-recycles
  **Year of data:** page modified 2025. **Opened today: yes**

- **National hauler guidance (no number):**
  **Say it:** "WM lists food and liquids among the most common recycling contaminants, and tells people to compost or trash soggy paper products."
  **Sources and URLs:**
  WM "Most Common Contaminants" poster, 2026: https://www.wm.com/content/dam/wm/documents/RecyclingResources/Posters-Guides-Tools/Poster-Most-Common-Contaminants-093026.pdf
  WM "Recycling 101": https://www.wm.com/recycle-right/recycling-101
  **Year of data:** 2026. **Opened today: yes**

- **Number:** 10% did not know a pizza box must be emptied; 15% more did not know food cannot go in recycling.
  **Say it:** "In a Recycling Partnership survey of 1,400 people, 77% said they always empty their pizza boxes, 10% did not know they needed to, and 15% more did not know food cannot go in recycling."
  **Source:** The Recycling Partnership, "Studying the Recyclability of Pizza Boxes", July 16, 2020.
  **URL:** https://recyclingpartnership.org/recyclability-of-pizza-boxes/
  **Year of data:** published 2020. **Opened today: yes**

- **Careful with greasy pizza boxes: the official answers differ.**
  **Say it:** "The EPA says pizza boxes can be recycled even if they have grease in them, as long as the food is out. The City of Tampa does not accept pizza boxes or soiled food boxes in its recycling. So grease rules depend on the program, while none of these sources accept leftover food in recycling."
  **Sources and URLs:**
  US EPA, "How Do I Recycle Common Recyclables" (last updated May 19, 2026): https://www.epa.gov/recycle/how-do-i-recycle-common-recyclables
  City of Tampa brochure, 03-2026: https://www.tampa.gov/sites/default/files/document/2026/recycling-brochure-2026-03.pdf
  **Year of data:** 2026. **Opened today: yes**
