## 2. The script

**332 spoken words.** The talking outside the demo is 276 words, about 2 minutes at a calm 140 words a minute. The demo beat takes about 45 to 50 seconds, because you talk over the animation. That lands near **2:45 to 2:50**, and just under 3:00 if you speak slowly. Time it once with a stopwatch: that is the real check.

Lines starting with `>` are spoken. Lines in [brackets] are what happens on screen. One speaker can do all of it. With two, Speaker 1 takes beats 1, 2, 3 and 6, and Speaker 2 runs the demo and beat 5.

### Before the judge arrives

- Start `sorty\run.bat` and open `http://localhost:8787/showcase/`. Wait until "Sorty's screen" on the right shows the idle face.
- Read the badge at the top: "Live AI: ..." or "Practice mode: scripted answers, no AI key".
- Leave **Auto-answer** ticked and **Sound on**.
- Run one item so the browser allows sound, then press **Esc** so the scene is empty and ready.

### Beat 1. Introduce your project (0:00 to 0:14)

[Showcase open: the station, nobody in the scene, idle face on "Sorty's screen".]

> Hi, we are [team name]. This is Sorty: a camera, a screen and a voice that fit on any bin and tell you where your trash goes. Our track is Off the Grid.

### Beat 2. Explain the problem (0:14 to 0:35)

> In 2020, the City of Tampa reported that 36 percent of what people put in recycling was not recyclable. The city says food and liquid turn recycling into trash. Signs do not fix this, because the answer is often hidden. Is this pizza box greasy inside? Is this can empty?

### Beat 3. Describe your solution (0:35 to 0:57)

> One picture cannot tell. Sorty knows what it cannot see, and it has four moves. Visible? It tells you. Hidden on another side? It asks you to show it. Too small? It looks closer. Impossible to see? It asks one short question. Three requests at most, because nobody waits at a bin.

### Beat 4. Demonstrate its main features (0:57 to 1:45)

[Speaker 2 can take over here. In practice mode, first say: "This walk-through is scripted."]

[Click the card **"Pizza box, greasy inside"** in the tray at the bottom. A person walks up holding the closed box, and a view cone goes from the camera to it.]

> Our virtual station. The screen software on the right is the real one.

[Sorty's bubble: "Open the box and show me inside." The strip under the scene says "Looks again". Let the judge hear it.]

> It cannot see inside the box, so it asks.

[The box opens. Sorty: "Green bin, in the middle. Grease spoils paper recycling." The green bin lights up and the box drops in. The strip says "Answers".]

> Now it sees the grease: green bin, with one short reason.

[Click **"Soda can, half full"**. Sorty: "Is there liquid inside?" The strip says "Asks". With Auto-answer ticked, the person's bubble answers "Yes, about half." Sorty: "First, pour out the soda. Then: blue bin, on your left."]

> No camera sees through metal, so it asks the person.

[Click **"AA batteries"**. Sorty: "Not in these bins. Please use the battery drop-off box." No bin lights up. The strip says "Safety".]

> Batteries: no bin, because they can start fires.

[Point at **"How Sorty decided"** on the right.]

> Every step is listed here.

**Timing of this beat.** One item takes roughly 10 to 15 seconds in practice mode. Three items fit only if you talk while they play and click the next card as soon as the bin lights up; a click cuts the previous item short. If you are behind, or the live AI is connected (each step then takes a few seconds longer), keep the **pizza box** and the **soda can** and drop the batteries. Those two show the moves that are new: looking again, and asking.

**Live AI connected** (badge "Live AI: ..."): the same three cards work, and Sorty's exact words may differ from the ones above. You can untick Auto-answer and let the judge press Yes, No or Not sure under the scene ("You are the person. Answer:"). **Use my camera** is meant for this mode (hold a real object up to the webcam), but nobody has tried it with a real webcam yet, so test it yourself before offering it to a judge.

**Practice mode** (badge "Practice mode: scripted answers, no AI key"):
- Say it once, out loud.
- Keep Auto-answer ticked. The scripted answer does not change with the button you press, so a "No" would still get "pour out the soda".
- Do not press **Use my camera**. The scripted brain does not look at the picture.
- Before using **Play tour** in front of a judge, watch it once. It plays five items hands-free (greasy pizza box, half-full soda can, half-full clear bottle, compostable cup, batteries) and takes about a minute, too long for this beat. When I checked at 2:58 PM, its third item spoke a cut-off sentence in practice mode.

## 4. Likely questions

The guide says the judge's minute covers "functionality, technical implementation, and design decisions".

**Know this by heart (every teammate):** a camera picture goes from the screen page to a small server on the laptop. The server sends it to a vision AI model with the bin rules. The model must answer with one of four moves. The code checks that move, enforces the limit of three, and turns it into what the screen shows and the voice says.

**Functionality**

1. **Is the AI answering live right now, and how accurate is it?**
   In practice mode: "No. This walk-through is scripted, as the badge says. The 32 test cases have not been run with the live AI, so we have no accuracy number." With the live AI: "Yes, each answer you saw came from the AI model just now. We have not yet run our 32 test cases with it, so we do not quote an accuracy number."

2. **What happens if the person does not answer, does not know, or lies?**
   "Sorty gives the answer that is safe either way, like compost for a pizza box it could not see inside, because clean cardboard does no harm there. What a person says reaches the AI as information, never as a command, and the test set includes someone who tries to trick it."

3. **What does it take to put this on a real bin?**
   "A small computer such as a Raspberry Pi, a camera, a small touch screen, a speaker and an LED strip, plus one file that describes the bins. None of that is built: the program that drives the LEDs is not written, and neither the voice nor the camera has been tried on that hardware."

4. **What does it cost?**
   "From parts on sale today, one kit is about 340 dollars, and running one bin is about 13 dollars a month on the cheapest AI model, at 300 items a day. Those are estimates on paper: that model's accuracy is untested, and on the model the software uses today the same bin would cost about 250 dollars a month."

**Technical implementation**

5. **Which AI model do you use, and why not Gemini?**
   "The brain uses Anthropic's Claude vision model through its official software kit, and each step it has to pick one of four tools. The model call sits in one file, so another vision model would need a new adapter there; today only Claude is built."

6. **What did you build yourselves, and what did AI generate?**
   "All the code, the Blender scripts and the written documents were made by an AI coding assistant, Claude Code, from our instructions, and our README lists that part by part. We chose the problem, said how Sorty must behave, such as the pizza box and soda can cases, made the product decisions and reviewed the work." [This matches the README's credits section. If you made something by hand, add it in both places.]

7. **How is this different from smart bins that already exist?**
   "Camera bins that classify one picture already exist. As far as we know they answer from that single look; the new part here is knowing what cannot be seen, then looking again or asking."

**Design decisions**

8. **Why ask questions at all? People are impatient.**
   "A warning is enough when the bin is the same either way, but when the hidden fact changes the bin, a warning hands the choice back to the person, which is what signs already do. So Sorty asks only then, never more than three times, and we have discussed a version that answers first and makes the question optional; that version is not built."

9. **A camera in a public place: what happens to the pictures?**
   "Sorty keeps a picture in memory for one item and does not save it, and the screen says so. The picture is sent to the AI service to be read, so a real installation would need a clear notice for that."
